## Cumulative experimental findings

### Retained as architectural knowledge

- one active Observation → Situation Assessment → Operational Picture → Decision → Commitment → Control path is preferable to parallel authority paths;
- Native Continuation evidence, Future Space provenance and structured traces are useful inputs;
- capability effectiveness and operational sufficiency are different conclusions;
- a settled refuge may require another manoeuvre leg without creating a new Commitment;
- Leader/Follower responsibility is Decision-relevant Knowledge;
- unresolved responsibility must survive capability completion and intent changes;
- Control outcomes should report physical facts rather than reinterpret the Encounter;
- deterministic replay is required before live authority.

### Rejected as governing implementation

- procedural action fallthrough as a substitute for an explicit Candidate Action Space;
- a Commitment Ledger that records requested transitions without enforcing legality;
- Situation Assessment pre-selecting one authoritative refuge candidate;
- generic fallback from unavailable Hold to Leader reposition;
- Intent Expiry as proof of Commitment completion;
- module-local smoke tests as proof of system composition;
- Prototype 16 as the permanent unit of Control decomposition.

## Terminal evidence from v4.6.77

The v4.6.77 TS015 trace demonstrated an illegal lifecycle transition:

1. Native Reposition and Native Handover completed.
2. Closure responsibility remained unresolved.
3. Decision requested Commitment completion while `safeRelease=false`.
4. The Ledger accepted completion.
5. A new Encounter and Commitment were admitted against the same unresolved recovery relationship.

This disproved the hypothesis that architecture-shaped procedural modules were sufficient. The failure is classified as the **Architectural Constraint Enforcement Gap**, reinforced by the **Composition Validation Gap**.
